# Presentation visual design

1. Base fonts and colors on the creator's existing content and any logo/fonts they supply; otherwise choose a fitting palette and free fonts and say so. Preserve exact logo geometry and proportions.
2. Use a consistent slide grid, margins, title placement, footer logic, spacing scale and type hierarchy.
3. Prioritize one dominant visual idea per slide. Favor large photography, bold short headlines, compelling diagrams and meaningful negative space.
4. Avoid tiny text, overly dense layouts, stock-photo clichés, gratuitous gradients and random transitions.
5. Design for projected display as well as laptop viewing. Maintain accessible contrast and legible labels.
6. Reuse approved assets and templates intelligently without making all slides identical.
7. Ensure images are high resolution, cropped intentionally and not distorted. Preserve authentic appearances.
8. Use animations only when they improve sequencing or comprehension; do not add effects by default.
9. Keep all slides editable when the requested output supports it; avoid rasterizing all content.
10. When designing for another brand or series, use its specific identity alongside the creator's own style.
