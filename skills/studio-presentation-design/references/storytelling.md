# Narrative and slide architecture

Build a clear argument, not a sequence of information dumps. A typical persuasive deck:
1. High-impact title / promise
2. Why this matters now
3. The problem or opportunity
4. Concept / solution
5. Audience and distinctive value
6. Proof points / track record
7. Proposed collaboration or plan
8. Deliverables and activation
9. Results / economics (if sourced)
10. Next steps / call to action

Adapt to the request; do not force every deck into this outline. Start each slide with a takeaway headline rather than a vague topic label. Use one key message per slide, progressive disclosure, intentional transitions and a strong conclusion. Place detailed supporting material in an appendix when appropriate.

For sponsorship decks: differentiate rights, inventory, deliverables, measurable value, assumptions, and pricing. Do not imply commitments or guaranteed outcomes not supported by source materials.
